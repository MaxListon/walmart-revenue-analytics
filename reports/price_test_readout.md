# Household price test: readout (simulated result)

> **This is a simulation, not a real test.** A known demand response was planted into real FY2016 Household sales to show the analysis and decision rule, written in advance, working end to end. See [the experiment brief](../docs/price_test_brief.md).

**Setup:** 10,175 product-store pairs (1,044 Household products in 10 stores), half randomly priced +5% for 12 weeks after an 8-week baseline. Model: Poisson, product-store + store-week + product-week fixed effects, errors clustered by product.

| Planted elasticity | Estimated | 95% CI | p (vs −1) | Units, treated vs control | Revenue change at estimate | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| −0.40 | **−0.35** | −0.76 to 0.07 | 0.001 | −0.9% | +3.2% | **Roll out** |
| −1.00 | **−0.94** | −1.36 to −0.53 | 0.397 | −3.8% | +0.3% | **Don't roll out** |

**Reading it:** when the price rise truly pays (planted −0.4), the estimate lands near −0.4 and is significantly above −1, so the rule says roll out. When it doesn't (planted −1.0), the rule says don't. The decision follows the rule set in advance, not the point estimate alone.

**Is the rule reliable?** Repeating each case with 40 different random assignments: when the rise truly pays, the rule said roll out **82%** of the time (the design targets about 89% power); when it doesn't, it wrongly said roll out **0%** of the time (the target is at most 5%). With 40 runs these rates are only accurate to about ±12 points, so they are consistent with the design. The estimates are unbiased: they average −0.43 and −1.03 against planted values of −0.40 and −1.00.
