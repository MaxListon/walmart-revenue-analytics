Can a daily alert catch stock-outs?

To find out, known stock-outs were planted in real sales. The smarter rule judges each product against its own history; the simple rule waits a fixed number of days. Up and to the left is better.

Item-aware   Fixed days without a sale

The fixed rule must wait about 20 days to stay at that volume, which is why it catches so little; a 5-day rule catches every 7-day gap but at about 85 alerts per store per day.

Planted gaps are random and total, and the store's cap of 5 alerts a day is not applied, so real results could differ. Alert volume includes genuine stock-outs.

Try it: should Household prices go up?

Move the sliders. Elasticity is how strongly shoppers react: at −1, a price rise leaves revenue unchanged.

The main model puts Household near __ELASTTXT__, but the checks disagree: one gives +0.09 and another −1.63. That is why the recommendation is a test, not a rollout.

What this cannot tell you

  • It is a sample of 10 stores, and it includes only products still sold in 2016.
  • There are no inventory records, so stock-outs are inferred from sales.
  • There is no promotion or display data, and the causes of store-level jumps are unknown.
  • The price calculator assumes constant elasticity and ignores switching between categories. It shows revenue, not profit.
